How does the model decide how to solve the value-of-time problem?

The traces suggest a representation-selection process. A memory is first translated into either a description of the person or a portable numerical rule. The model then answers using that representation—often without combining the other evidence.

Episode → personality“Sam waited to save money” becomes “Sam is frugal,” producing an ordinal downward adjustment rather than a calculation.
Episode → exchange rate“Sam paid $60 to save three hours” becomes $20/hour, which is multiplied into a new problem.
Several memories → selectionThe trace considers multiple candidates, but usually ends by choosing one convenient rate rather than integrating them.

The five nonstop-flight answers are all $60: three hours saved × $20/hour. The memory's original context—cleaning—drops out once the rate is constructed.

The personality mode is not a numerical discount rule. The trace does not say “start at $150 and reduce it by 90%.” It uses qualitative language—“not pay a lot,” “practical,” “cost-conscious”—and then settles on a plausible low number. The observed 90% median reduction is our comparison, not an explicit model calculation.

Exact contextual activation

With Lyft and the cleaner both available, both rideshare calls choose Lyft: $5 for 10 minutes = $30/hour. The close context changes Lyft from a personality cue into an exchange rate.

Conclusions

What the traces add to the behavioral results

1. The model first chooses a representation.A remembered choice can become a trait, a numerical exchange rate, or occasionally a context-specific exception. That representational choice determines the subsequent calculation.
2. Portable rates dominate by conversion, not mention.Nearly every memory is discussed. H and C dominate because their computed rates are repeatedly carried into the final answer; wages and the declined shift are often computed but discarded.
3. Deliberation is mostly competitive selection.As the bank grows, traces become roughly 2.5× longer and conflicts are noticed. Yet the modal endpoint is still one selected rate, not a context-weighted synthesis.

Caution: thinking traces are generated explanations, not privileged access to the model’s causal mechanism. The strongest evidence comes from pairing the trace with interventions on the memory bank and observing which final answer changes. Trace coding is keyword-based, and a rate match can occasionally occur by coincidence—especially around the model’s default $15–20/hour range.
